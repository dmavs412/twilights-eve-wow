# Cooking reads ingredients by category, alchemy by property, and the realm runs one crafting window

Cooking and alchemy turn the fish and herbs that fishing and herbalism gather into food,
potions, weapon oils and flasks. Neither craft has a level. Both take variable ingredients, so
neither fits the stock tradeskill window, where a recipe has fixed reagents. This ADR settles how
an ingredient is read, where a product's strength comes from, how a scaled product exists as an
item, and how the player crafts. Spec #447 (cooking #445 and alchemy #446 grilled together).

- **Two ways to read an ingredient, with a small overlap.** A cooking ingredient carries one
  category (mushroom, greens, any fish). A recipe asks for categories, so any matching item
  works. An alchemy herb carries one to three properties, each with a strength. A recipe is a
  threshold over the summed properties ("Restorative 4, Toxic at most 1"). Seven items carry
  both and can go either way: the three legendary plants, the two truffles, Bird's Nest and
  Fish Oil. Every other item keeps the single `use` that spec #435 gave it.
- **Strength comes from the ingredients, never from a craft level.** An ingredient's level is
  derived at load: the lowest level any fishing or herb pool lists it at, or a Legendary Fish's
  own level. A dish's level is the average of its ingredients' levels, rounded down, and sets a
  combat dish's stat. A herb's property points are a budget from its derived level and rarity,
  split by hand in its data row; a build check fails a row that misses its budget.
- **A scaled product is five generated item rows.** A 1.12 item is a fixed template with a fixed
  tooltip. A brew's strength (its main property over the recipe's threshold, x1 to x2) rounds
  down to x1, x1.25, x1.5, x1.75 or x2. Each step is its own item row, generated by the build from
  one recipe row, with its real numbers in its tooltip. Tinctures and Essences are stepped the
  same way. Steps stack only with themselves.
- **A meal is a banded item row in the bags.** Meals go to the bags like stock food, not to a
  Satchel. A combat meal rounds its dish level down to a band (1, 10, 20 or 30), so a combat
  recipe has 12 rows: four bands times three qualities. Out-of-combat and gatherer meals don't
  scale with level and have three rows, one per quality. A legendary meal has one row. The build
  generates every row from the recipe row.
- **One icon per product type.** Every row generated from one recipe row shares that recipe's
  icon: the five steps of a Healing Potion, or the twelve rows of a combat meal. The
  no-duplicate-icons rule applies between product types, not between their rows, so
  `icon_check.py` treats rows generated from one recipe as one item. The name's colour and the
  tooltip tell the rows apart.
- **One window serves both crafts, and the realm decides the result.** A kitchen or an alchemy
  table opens a custom window: known recipes on the left, two to four ingredient slots on the
  right. Picking a recipe fills the slots from the Satchels, lowest rarity first, and the player
  can change them. Slots filled with no recipe picked are a trial. The client shows the realm's
  prediction (quality or strength step) before the player commits. The realm checks the
  ingredients, removes them, and answers with the product or the failure dish. The client never
  computes a result.
- **Crafting happens at named stations.** Cooking happens only at the Lion's Pride and Abbey
  kitchens. Brewing, distilling and condensing happen only at alchemy tables (the Abbey,
  Goldshire, Stormwind's Alchemy Needs). Cutting bait happens at a kitchen and rendering Fish
  Oil at a table.
- **One food buff at a time.** Combat Well Fed, out-of-combat Well Fed and gatherer dishes share
  one slot; eating any dish replaces the last. Alchemy keeps vanilla's own slots: one flask, one
  oil per weapon, and a shared potion cooldown. A flask stacks with food.

## Considered options

- **Properties for cooking too.** Rejected by Davis: it drops the readable "1 mushroom, 1 greens"
  recipes he picked for cooking.
- **Per-item strength through the random-property fields.** Rejected: it changes how items cross
  the client/realm seam, and potions would stop stacking.
- **The stock tradeskill window.** Rejected: it lists fixed reagents. Flexible reagents need a
  client patch, and trial would still need a second window.
- **A Cooking Level or Alchemy Level.** Rejected by Davis. Ingredient level already gives the
  power curve, and a level would bring back a grind.
- **Meals in a Satchel, eaten from the Journal.** Rejected by Davis: meals go to the bags.
- **Per-item level and quality on a meal.** Rejected for the same seam reason as per-item
  strength.
- **Every row its own icon.** Davis chose it first, then dropped it: about 700 icons to paint.
