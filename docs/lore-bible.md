## The story in one page

The world is being erased by a grey mist, and the Stormwind pocket is the last place it hasn't
reached. The mist is a weapon, run by the Twilight's Hammer and the Black Dragonflight. Its true
maker is Deathwing, but that's a secret for writers only: an extremely late reveal, if it's ever
revealed at all. Every story beat in the game is part of the fight over the machines that pull it
in.

**The mist.** It's the Twisting Nether's grey edge, dragged into the world by **Anchors**: machines
forged from tuned elementium, the resonant ore that rings when struck. Whatever the mist covers is
cut out of the world. Nothing has ever come back out, so everyone assumes what lies behind it is
dead. Ordinary people call its silence **the Hush**.

**The last pocket.** Lordaeron, Northrend, Kalimdor, Westfall and the Great Sea are already gone.
Elwynn, Stormwind, Redridge, Duskwood and the Burning Steppes remain, because the Anchors here
aren't finished. When the ring closes, the pocket goes too.

**Who's building it.** The Twilight's Hammer mines the ore and builds the Anchors. Onyxia, as Lady
Prestor, keeps Stormwind's court blind. Nefarian holds Blackrock, where the last Anchor will close
the ring. Morganth is the only engineer who can tune the ore. Lord Cedric Valemont, a refugee from
Lordaeron, pays for it all because he believes finished Anchors will make the mist a wall around
Stormwind instead of a tide.

**Who wants to steal it.** The mist swallowed Northrend and froze the Lich King on his throne. One
Necropolis, Mourncrown, was hanging over Duskwood when the pocket closed. Its lich, Sareth Coldvein,
is free of the Helm for the first time. He wants an Anchor of his own, so that he decides who is cut
out of the world.

**The three-way endgame.** Onyxia and Nefarian want the ring closed. Sareth wants it captured. The
players want the Anchors broken. Every Anchor broken pushes the mist back, which is how new land
(starting with the Burning Steppes) opens up.

## The mist and the Anchors

The mist is mechanical. It has no mind, no voice and no will. People and creatures make it, feed it
and point it.

**Elementium (resonant ore).** Deathwing seeded seams of it in the deep ages, long before humans
settled Elwynn. It still carries his corruption, which is why it hums. Struck, it rings; held to the
ear, it sounds wrong. Raw ore does nothing on its own.

**Anchors.** An Anchor is a forged and tuned mass of elementium, set into the ground and fed. It
pulls the Nether's grey edge into the world around it. Each Anchor holds one stretch of mist, and
whoever holds the Anchor holds that stretch. An Anchor needs:

- **Ore,** dug from seams under Elwynn, Duskwood and Blackrock.
- **Tuning,** which only Morganth knows how to do properly. Untuned Anchors leak, fail or make
  something worse (see Duskwood).
- **Tribute,** a steady feed of material and life. Relics, timber, livestock and people all go in.
  This is what moves upriver from Jerod's Landing.
- **A site,** surveyed and marked in advance. The cult paints the hammer-and-black-sun on cairns,
  crates and stones where an Anchor will stand.

**What the mist does.** It doesn't kill visibly. It cuts out. Sound stops, roads end in grey, and
anyone who walks in doesn't walk out. At the edge, animals go quiet first, then the wind, then
people's voices. The border tells players: "You cannot go that way."

**What it does to the undead.** The undead aren't cut out. They freeze. A ghoul that wanders
into the grey stops mid-step and stays there, preserved and motionless. The Scourge outside the
pocket weren't destroyed; whole armies stand frozen in the mist, and the Lich King sits frozen on
his throne in Northrend. This is why Sareth wants an Anchor of his own rather than the mist itself:
uncontrolled, it would freeze his army too.

**The Hush (folk speech).** Ordinary people call the mist's silence "the Hush." "The Hush took
Westfall." "It went quiet, the way the Hush does." It's a word for the effect, not a creature.
Characters can be wrong about it; the cult encourages people to think of it as a god.

**Pushing it back.** Breaking an Anchor releases its stretch of mist, and the grey rolls back to the
next standing Anchor. This is the in-world reason new land opens. At launch, breaking the Redridge
Anchor at Ilgalar clears the pass into the Burning Steppes. Breaking an Anchor safely takes
knowledge only Morganth had, which is why players need his failure notes, translated by Toren,
before they can do it. Westfall and the Great Sea stay sealed until later Anchors fall.

## History up to the game's start

The game's history follows classic Warcraft until the Third War, then diverges when the mist
appears. Nobody alive saw where it began. The last column says what ordinary people in the pocket
believe, so quest writers know what a farmer or guard can say.

| When | What actually happened | What people in the pocket believe |
|---|---|---|
| The deep ages | Deathwing seeds elementium seams under the Eastern Kingdoms. | Nothing. It's just ore that rings. |
| About 25 years ago | The First War. Stormwind falls and is rebuilt. | Canon history; everyone knows it. |
| About 20 years ago | The Second War. Deathwing betrays the Horde, then vanishes. | He's dead or gone. |
| About 12 years ago | The Twilight's Hammer's first test Anchor, buried in Duskwood, is untuned. It doesn't make mist; it makes night. Duskwood never fully sees day again. | Duskwood is cursed. Nobody knows why. |
| About 7 years ago | The Plague of Undeath and the Third War. Lordaeron falls to the Scourge. | Canon history; refugees tell it. |
| About 5 years ago | Grey mist appears on Lordaeron's northern horizon and rolls south, swallowing the Scourge with everything else. Lord Cedric Valemont flees ahead of it. | The north is gone. Some say the mist stopped the plague. |
| About 4 years ago | Cedric reaches Stormwind and is welcomed as a hero. The cult approaches him through intermediaries. | Cedric is a brave survivor and a generous patron. |
| About 3 years ago | The Necropolis Mourncrown arrives over Duskwood to harvest its graves. | The Scourge has come south. Darkshire is terrified. |
| About 2 years ago | The mist reaches Northrend. The Lich King is frozen on his throne. Mourncrown's lich, Sareth Coldvein, wakes free. | Nobody in the pocket knows the Lich King is frozen. |
| About 1 year ago | King Varian sails for a summit and his ship vanishes into the grey. Bolvar becomes regent, with Lady Prestor at his side. Westfall and the Great Sea are swallowed. | The king is missing, not dead. The mist is "weather" according to the court. |
| Months ago | The cult takes Jasperlode and pays kobolds to dig Echo Ridge toward the Abbey. Morganth sets his prototype rod in the Tower of Azora. | Kobold trouble. The tower mage is eccentric. |
| Days ago | Northshire Abbey falls in one night. The game begins. | The Abbey fell and nobody knows how. |

Dates are approximate and relative to the game's start, so they can be adjusted to match any
timeline you settle on later.

## Villains and factions

Every villain wants something different from the mist, and each one is using someone else. Write
their quests and notes so that the motive shows, even when the villain is offscreen.

| Who | What they want | What they do in the pocket | What they don't know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deathwing | To unmake a world that refused him. | Nothing directly. He's never seen and almost never mentioned. At most, an extremely late reveal. |  |
| Twilight's Hammer | To serve the end of the world, which they treat as a holy act. | Mine ore, build and feed Anchors, mark sites, pay locals in Twilight coin. | That Nefarian plans to keep the pocket rather than let it go grey. |
| Lady Katrana Prestor (Onyxia) | Stormwind's army trapped and its throne hers. | Keeps the court blind. Falsifies dispatches ("Elwynn considered clear"). Shields Cedric from scrutiny. Has Cedric killed. | That Sareth is courting Morganth. |
| Nefarian | The pocket as his larder once it's sealed, with the Blackrock as its new masters. | Holds Blackrock, the site of the ring's last Anchor. Built the breach at Blackrock Stronghold so the Horde can move in and out. | How close the players are to the Anchors. |
| Morganth | Credit for his genius, which Theocritus and Dalaran never gave him. | Tunes every Anchor. Set the Azora rod. Works from the Tower of Ilgalar. | That the cult will dispose of him once the ring is closed. |
| Lord Cedric Valemont | To never watch a city die again. | Funds the cult through intermediaries. Gives it noble cover. Recruited Morganth. | That finished Anchors close the pocket instead of protecting it. |
| Sareth Coldvein | To become what the Lich King only meant to be. | Rules Mourncrown. Digs Duskwood's seam for his own Anchor. Courts Morganth. | That the players can break Anchors, which threatens his plan as much as the cult's. |

**Lord Cedric Valemont, the frightened helper.** He fled Lordaeron ahead of the mist and watched it
swallow the Scourge whole. To him it looked like salvation. The cult told him that finished Anchors
would turn the mist into a wall around Stormwind. He's sincere, generous and terrified, and he has
never once asked where the tribute goes. He dies at the Cathedral of Light: confronted by the
players, he gives up the Anchor locations, and Prestor's agents kill him before he can testify to
the regent.

**Sareth Coldvein, the heir apparent.** He studied necromancy in Dalaran alongside Kel'Thuzad and
always believed he was the better scholar. He joined the Cult of the Damned for the Lich King's
favour and never got it. When the Helm went silent, Sareth woke with his own will. He needs a
working Anchor and Morganth's knowledge of how to tune one. A suggested closing line: "He was never
worthy of it. Neither am I. But I was there to take it."

**Morganth, the man everyone wants.** He was Theocritus's apprentice and thought dead. He's
brilliant, proud and bitter, and he believes he's the mastermind. Sareth's letters offer him the one
thing the cult won't: credit. He dies as the final boss of the Tower of Ilgalar, killed by the
players. His notebooks survive, and they're what the cult and Sareth fight over afterwards, because
without him nobody can tune a new Anchor cleanly.

**Toren, the one Morganth spared.** After Azora, Toren goes looking for Morganth, and the cult
catches him. Morganth orders him kept alive and unharmed, because Toren is the one person he still
cares about. That's why the cult "kept" him at Ridgepoint rather than paying or killing him.

**The cipher and the payoff.** Morganth wrote his notebooks in a cipher the two of them invented as
apprentices. With Morganth dead, Toren is the only person alive who can read them. Every notebook
page players find is unreadable until Toren translates it, which keeps sending players back to him,
and it makes him a target for both the cult and Sareth from the 40s onward.

**Morganth's notebooks.** The notebooks hold the how of the Anchors. Cedric gives up the where at
the Cathedral, so the notebooks contain no map. They have four parts:

| Part | What it is | Who ends up with it |
|---|---|---|
| The tuning method | How to turn raw elementium into a working Anchor. | The cult, who escape with it when Ilgalar falls. |
| The failure notes | Records of the Duskwood test and other mistakes, showing how an Anchor breaks. | The players. It's the in-world reason they learn to push the mist back. |
| The casing specs | Dark Iron forge designs for the casings. With them, Nefarian wouldn't need the Dark Iron to finish the last Anchor. | Sareth's agents, who take them in the fight at Ilgalar. They're Sareth's leverage over Nefarian. |
| The unsent letter | A personal letter to Toren, scattered across the last pages. | The players, who carry it to Toren. |

**Torn three ways.** The notebooks are torn apart in the fight at Ilgalar. Every faction escapes
with pages it can't read, because they're all in the cipher. That drives the page race and the
collectible hunt in the 30-50 zones, and it puts a price on Toren's head.

**Sareth comes for Toren.** Sareth holds the casing specs but can't read them. His agents hunt Toren
through the 40s, and eventually take him to Mourncrown. Rescuing Toren becomes a level-60 arc inside
the Necropolis.

**The road to the Steppes.** The notebooks, Toren and the Burning Steppes form one chain:

1. **Ilgalar (34-38).** Players kill Morganth and recover the failure notes. The Anchor keeps
   running.
2. **Through the 40s.** Toren translates the failure notes page by page as players find more of
   them. Each translation teaches a little more about how an Anchor breaks.
3. **Around 48-50.** With the notes complete, players return to Ilgalar and break the Anchor. The
   mist rolls off the pass and the Burning Steppes open.

This is also why the cult and Sareth want Toren so badly: he's the reason the players can break
Anchors at all.

**Minor factions:**

- **The Defias** are local criminals paid in Twilight coin. Most don't know what they're helping.
  The Defias in Westfall were lost with it.
- **The Riverpaw gnolls** dig and fight for the cult because Morganth promised Hogger power.
- **The Blackrock orcs** are Nefarian's muscle in Redridge and the Steppes.
- **The kobolds** are victims. The cult drove them out of Jasperlode, and Grik's people want only to
  leave.

## Zone arcs and dungeons

Each zone has one job in the overarching story. Elwynn is the quarry, Redridge is the Anchor,
Duskwood is the warning, Stormwind is the court, and the Burning Steppes are the dragons' side.

| Zone | Levels | Role in the story | Dungeons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elwynn Forest | 1-30 | The quarry: where the ore is dug and the tribute gathered. | Northshire Abbey, Jasperlode Mine, Tower of Azora, Forest's Edge (built or designed) |
| Redridge Mountains | 30-50 | The Anchor: Morganth's workshop and the Blackrock front. | Tower of Ilgalar, Stonewatch Keep |
| Duskwood | 30-50 | The warning: the first failed Anchor and the Scourge. | Mistmantle Manor, Raven Hill Deeps; Mourncrown at 60 |
| Stormwind City | Hub, 30-60 | The court: politics, cover-ups and Prestor. | The Stockade, the Deeprun Tram, the Cathedral of Light, Stormwind Keep |
| Burning Steppes and Blackrock | 50-60 | The dragons' side: the last Anchor site. | Blackrock Stronghold, Dreadmaul Rock, plus Blackrock Mountain |

### Elwynn Forest (1-30): the quarry

Elwynn sits on the richest elementium seam in the pocket. The cult digs it, ships it upriver and
gathers tribute here. Everything already built stays, reframed: the Abbey fell because it stood over
the seam, Jasperlode is the cult's main mine, the Azora rod was Morganth's first working Anchor, and
Jerod's Landing is the shipping point. The Tribute crate marked for Ilgalar holds a forged Anchor
core, the heart of the Redridge Anchor. Elwynn has no high-level content of its own. Later quests
send players back for evidence, witnesses and old threads.

### Redridge and Duskwood (30-50): paired zones

Players move back and forth between these two zones, passing through Stormwind, the way classic WoW
moves players between Redridge, Duskwood and the city. Quest chains should hand off across zones,
and level ranges overlap rather than running in sequence.

**Redridge: the Anchor.** The Tower of Ilgalar is the eastern Anchor, and Morganth works there. The
Blackrock orcs are Nefarian's muscle, and their working alongside the cult is the first hint of the
dragons. Magistrate Solomon's "worse problems" are real. Players kill Morganth at Ilgalar in their
30s, but the Anchor keeps running. They return around 48-50, armed with Toren's translation of the
failure notes, to break it and clear the pass into the Burning Steppes.

- **Tower of Ilgalar (about 34-38).** Morganth's workshop and a working Anchor. Morganth is the
  final boss and dies here. Players find Sareth's letters among his notebooks. The Anchor survives
  his death; a running machine doesn't need its tuner. Players recover the failure notes here and
  come back to break the Anchor around 48-50.
- **Stonewatch Keep (about 44-48).** The Blackrock have taken the keep, with black dragonkin among
  them. It proves the orcs and the cult answer to the same master.

**Duskwood: the warning.** Duskwood's endless night is the cult's first test Anchor, untuned and
buried twelve years ago. It didn't make mist; it made night and woke the dead. It's where players
learn what Anchors do when they go wrong. Mourncrown hangs over it, drawn by the graves.

- **Mistmantle Manor (about 32-36).** The household above the test Anchor went silent on the night
  it was switched on. It's a haunted-house dungeon and players' first look at what the mist does to
  people.
- **Raven Hill Deeps (about 44-48).** Sareth's cultists dig Duskwood's seam under the cemetery for
  his own Anchor. Players learn the undead aren't silenced, and see Mourncrown's shadow up close.

### Stormwind City (hub): the court

The city doesn't know it's sealed. Ships are "delayed by weather" and letters go unanswered. Regent
Bolvar gets clean reports from Prestor's office. The House of Nobles protects Cedric, and Prestor
protects the House. Players arrive with Dughan's evidence and run into politics. Dughan stays in
Goldshire, and his reports to the city keep disappearing in Prestor's office.

- **The Stockade (about 30-34).** A prison riot, with Defias inmates paid in Twilight coin. It's the
  natural place to resolve Hogger's capture.
- **The Deeprun Tram (about 38-42).** The tram to Ironforge now ends in grey. The cult uses its
  tunnels as the smuggling channel for tribute. Deep in, a tram car sits stopped halfway into the
  mist with its passengers frozen mid-step: the first time players see the mist from inside.
  Entrance: the tram station in the Dwarven District.
- **The Cathedral of Light (about 48-50).** Cedric takes sanctuary in the Cathedral when the players
  close in. He confesses, gives up the Anchor locations, and Prestor's agents kill him on
  consecrated ground before he can reach the regent. Entrance: the Cathedral doors.
- **Stormwind Keep (60).** Prestor reveals herself as Onyxia in the throne room, in front of Bolvar
  and the House of Nobles. Entrance: the Keep gate.

### Burning Steppes and Blackrock (50-60): the dragons' side

The zone is under mist until players break the Ilgalar Anchor around level 50. Behind it, players
find Nefarian's real plan: the breach at Blackrock Stronghold, a breach in the mist built so the
Blackrock Horde can come and go once the ring is closed. Cedric never knew it existed, and neither
does most of the cult.

- **Blackrock Stronghold (about 52-55).** The orcs' walled fort, with the breach in the mist inside
  it, held by black dragonkin and Blackrock. Entrance: the fort gate.
- **Dreadmaul Rock (about 55-58).** Ogres paid in tribute. It's where the tribute ends up.
- **Blackrock Mountain.** The classic layout (Blackrock Depths, Lower and Upper Blackrock Spire,
  Molten Core, Blackwing Lair). Nefarian holds the site of the ring's last Anchor in Blackwing Lair.

**Ragnaros and the Dark Iron: the war in the mountain.** The Dark Iron built the first Anchor
casings, and now they're at war with the Black Dragonflight because of it.

- **The casings.** Only Dark Iron forges, fed by the Molten Core's heat, run hot enough to work
  elementium. The cult commissioned the casings and paid in tribute, and the Dark Iron didn't ask
  what they were for. Every early Anchor, including Ilgalar's, carries a Dark Iron forge stamp,
  which is a clue players can find in Redridge.
- **The discovery.** The mist cuts out the elements along with everything else. As the world shrank,
  Ragnaros felt his tether to the Firelands thin and learned what the casings were for. He ordered
  the forges cold.
- **The war.** Nefarian still needs one more casing for the ring's last Anchor, and only the Dark
  Iron can make it. Dark Iron and fire elementals now fight Blackrock and black dragonkin throughout
  the mountain. This gives the canon feud between Molten Core and Blackwing Lair a reason.
- **Not allies.** Ragnaros doesn't want to save anyone. He wants the world intact so that it can
  still burn. Players can use the war against Nefarian, but the Dark Iron stay enemies.

**Moira, in the background.** Ironforge is behind the mist, so Moira may be the last Bronzebeard.
While the war rages, she quietly gathers the Dark Iron who never wanted the casings built. She stays
a background thread, surfacing through rumours, intercepted letters and a few quests, rather than
the main plot.

### Mourncrown (60): the Necropolis

Mourncrown is the last Scourge stronghold in the world and Sareth's seat. It uses a Naxxramas
interior across five dungeons. Each quarter is run by one of Kel'Thuzad's old lieutenants who now
serves a rival.

- **The Plague Quarter**
- **The Arachnid Quarter**
- **The Military Quarter**
- **The Construct Quarter**
- **The Crown Spire.** Sareth at the heart of the Necropolis, beside his half-built Anchor, wearing
  a crown he forged in imitation of the Helm.

## The reveal ladder

Players learn the truth one rung at a time. No quest, rumour or item text should reveal a rung
before the zone that owns it.

1. **"It's a Defias matter."** Elwynn, 10-13. Dughan refuses to believe McBride.
2. **"It's a cult."** Elwynn, 13-18. Jasperlode and the same mark, in the same hand.
3. **"The cult is building something."** Elwynn, 19-26. Azora's rod, Ridgepoint and the tribute
   moving upriver.
4. **"It's building machines, and the mist comes from them."** Redridge, 30s. The Tower of Ilgalar
   is a working Anchor.
5. **"Anchors can go wrong, and the dead aren't silenced."** Duskwood, 30s-40s. Mistmantle Manor and
   Raven Hill Deeps.
6. **"Someone at court is protecting the cult."** Stormwind, 40s. The falsified dispatch is traced
   to Prestor's office, and the money to Cedric.
7. **"The Anchors don't protect the pocket; they close it."** The Cathedral of Light, 48-50. Cedric
   learns the truth and dies.
8. **"The dragons are in it."** Stonewatch Keep and the Burning Steppes, 45-55. Dragonkin with the
   Blackrock, then the breach at Blackrock Stronghold.
9. **"Prestor is Onyxia."** Stormwind and Blackrock, 60.
10. **"Sareth wants the mist for himself."** Mourncrown, 60. The half-built Anchor in the Crown
    Spire.

Sareth can be glimpsed earlier (his letters at Ilgalar, his cultists at Raven Hill), but his goal
stays unclear until rung 10.

## Writer's rules

These rules keep quests, rumours and minor characters consistent with the larger story.

**Tone.** Grounded first. Every quest starts with a local, human problem (wolves at the fold, a
stolen ledger, a debtor who skipped town), and the larger story shows up in a detail. Villains are
rarely seen; their effects are. Dialogue is short and plain-spoken.

**Recurring motifs.** Use these to tie small content to the big story:

- **The mark.** The hammer and black sun, always "in the same hand." It marks cult property and
  future Anchor sites.
- **Ringing ore.** Elementium hums when struck. Smiths distrust it. People who handle it a lot
  dislike silence.
- **Twilight coin.** Strange coin nobody has seen before. It's how the cult pays locals.
- **Things going quiet.** Animals vanish before people do. Birds stop at the mist's edge. Candles
  that never go out (flame doesn't gutter where the air doesn't move).
- **Tribute going upriver.** Crates, relics, timber and livestock, always heading toward Everstill.
- **Missing news.** Delayed ships, unanswered letters and dispatches that say everything is fine.

**What the mist does (never contradict).**

- It's made by people and creatures. It has no mind and never speaks.
- Nothing comes back out. Nobody in the pocket knows for certain what's behind it.
- The undead aren't cut out; they freeze in place, preserved, for as long as the mist covers them.
- Breaking an Anchor pushes the mist back. Nothing else does.

**What ordinary people say.** They call it "the Hush" or "the grey." They disagree about it: some
think it stopped the plague, some think it's divine punishment, and the court says it's weather.
Characters can be wrong, but the narration and quest text never are.

**Never reveal early.**

- Deathwing, at all. Characters can talk about "whoever made the ore" or "something old," but nobody
  names him. He's an extremely late reveal, if ever, and no item text, dialogue or dungeon name
  points to him. The cult worships the Black Sun, its own mark, without ever saying what it stands
  for.
- Prestor's true identity, before level 60.
- That the Lich King is frozen. Sareth knows; nobody else in the pocket does.
- That Cedric is funding the cult, before the Stormwind chain in the 40s.

**Writing rumours.** A rumour is one local problem with one thread of the bigger story in it: one
marked crate, one Twilight coin, one witness who saw grey on the horizon. Never more than one thread
per rumour, and the thread must match the zone's rung on the reveal ladder.

## Changes to existing lore

Most of the existing Elwynn content fits as written. These are the lines that need a new reading or
a rewrite.

| Existing lore | Status | New reading |
|---|---|---|
| Grik: "what dug here dug for something older than a mine" | Keep | The seam was seeded by Deathwing in the deep ages. |
| The Eye Below uses the Eye of C'Thun model | Reframe | It's a cult creation: a creature grown around a lump of untuned ore to guard the dig. Its gaze silences the kobolds it looks at. The model can stay. |
| "The candles never went out" | Keep | Flame doesn't gutter near an Anchor, because the air there doesn't move. |
| Azora rod: "a signpost, not a prize" | Keep | The rod was Morganth's prototype Anchor. Its hum points to the next site. |
| The dead courier's dispatch: "Elwynn considered clear" | Expand | The dispatch was falsified in Prestor's office. The courier was killed because he saw the original. |
| "What the Tribute Was For": Morganth paid the cult to dig | Rewrite | Reverse it: the cult employs Morganth. The tribute feeds the Anchor at Ilgalar. |
| Hogger promised power "through Morganth" | Keep | Morganth needs a second shaft and gnoll labour. |
| Thalyssa Windbough, sent by Darnassus | Expand | Kalimdor is behind the mist. She's stranded, and doesn't know if Darnassus still exists. A strong personal hook. |
| Stockades "may not exist" | Resolved | The Stockade exists as a Stormwind dungeon, and it's where Hogger's capture turns in. |
| Westfall's border: "You cannot go that way" | Keep | Westfall is behind the mist. The Defias who were there are lost. |
